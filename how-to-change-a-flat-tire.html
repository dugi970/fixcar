How to Change a Flat Tire Safely (Step by Step)

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Pull over on firm, level ground away from traffic, switch on the hazard lights, apply the handbrake and loosen the wheel nuts before jacking. Raise the car only at the jacking points, swap the wheel, tighten the nuts by hand, lower the car and torque the nuts in a star pattern. Check the spare's pressure and re-check the nuts after a short drive.

Before you change the tyre

Safety matters more than speed. If you're on a motorway, a bend, a slope or soft ground, drive slowly on to a safer place if you can do so without causing more damage, or call roadside assistance. Never change a wheel with traffic close behind you.

  • Hazard lights on and, if you have one, a warning triangle placed behind the car
  • Everyone out of the car and away from traffic
  • Handbrake on, car in Park or first gear
  • Wheel chocks or a rock under the opposite wheel

What you need

  • Spare wheel with correct pressure
  • Jack and wheel brace (or lug wrench)
  • Locking wheel nut key, if fitted
  • Owner's manual, to find the jacking points
  • Gloves, torch and a small board for soft ground

How to change a flat tire, step by step

  1. Take off any wheel trim and loosen the wheel nuts about half a turn while the wheel is on the ground.
  2. Place the jack at the jacking point nearest the flat tyre, as shown in your manual.
  3. Raise the car until the flat tyre is just off the ground.
  4. Remove the nuts and take off the wheel.
  5. Fit the spare, lining up the holes, and thread the nuts on by hand.
  6. Tighten the nuts lightly in a star pattern.
  7. Lower the car, remove the jack and tighten the nuts firmly in a star pattern.
  8. Store the flat tyre and tools safely.

Safety note. Never put any part of your body under a car supported only by a jack. Never start the engine or let anyone sit inside while the car is raised. Use jack stands if you can for any extended work.

Tightening the nuts correctly

Tighten in a star pattern, not around in a circle. When you can, use a torque wrench set to the value in your manual, and re-check after 50 to 100 km (30 to 60 miles). See How to Tighten Lug Nuts Properly (Torque and Pattern).

After the change

  • Check the spare's pressure as soon as you can. See How to Check Tire Pressure Correctly.
  • If it's a space-saver spare, stay below the speed shown on the wheel (often around 80 km/h or 50 mph) and don't drive far.
  • Get the damaged tyre repaired or replaced soon.
  • Check the tread on the others. See Tire Tread Depth.
  • If the steering vibrates afterwards, see Steering Wheel Shakes at Highway Speed.

Common mistakes

  • Jacking at the wrong point, which can damage the car or let it fall
  • Loosening nuts only after the car is lifted
  • Forgetting to check the spare's pressure
  • Not re-torquing the nuts
  • Driving far or fast on a space-saver
  • Working on a slope or soft ground

FAQ

Can I drive on a flat tire?

Only very slowly and for a very short distance, and it can ruin the tyre and wheel. Change it or call for help.

Where do I put the jack?

At the jacking points shown in your owner's manual, usually on the reinforced sill near each wheel.

How long does it take to change a tire?

Around 15 to 30 minutes if you know what you're doing.

What if my wheel nuts won't loosen?

Use the full length of the brace, put your weight on it and, if needed, call for assistance rather than risk slipping.

Do I need to check the spare regularly?

Yes. Spares often lose pressure unnoticed, so check it every few months.
