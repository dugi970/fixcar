Grinding Noise When Braking: Causes and How Urgent It Is

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

A deep, constant grinding when you brake usually means the pads are worn down to the metal and are damaging the rotors. Stop driving and get the brakes inspected. A light grinding on the first stops after rain or a night outside can just be surface rust, and often disappears after a few stops.

Why your brakes grind

Brake pads are designed to wear. When the friction material is gone, the metal backing plate scrapes against the rotor. That makes a harsh grinding sound and can quickly score the rotor. Other causes are less serious, but a grinding noise is always worth checking.

Common causes

Worn-out pads

The most common reason. You may also have heard squealing earlier from the wear indicator. See Brake Pads vs Rotors.

Surface rust

After rain, a wash or a night outside, a thin layer of rust forms on the rotors and grinds off in the first few stops.

Stone or debris

A small stone caught between the pad and rotor can cause a scraping or grinding noise.

Seized caliper or slider pins

If a caliper sticks, the pad may drag, wear unevenly or overheat. You may notice a burning smell or the car pulling to one side.

Scored or damaged rotor

Deep grooves from old worn pads make noise, even with new pads fitted.

Wheel bearing

A grinding or humming noise that changes with speed or cornering and doesn't depend on braking may be a wheel bearing.

What to do now

  1. Avoid hard braking and long drives until the brakes are checked.
  2. Park safely and look through the wheel spokes at the pad thickness and the rotor surface.
  3. Check the brake fluid level. See How Often to Change Brake Fluid.
  4. If the pads are thin or you see deep grooves, replace pads and, where needed, rotors.
  5. If you're unsure, have a qualified mechanic inspect the car.

Safety note. Braking is a safety system. If the car pulls hard to one side, the pedal sinks, or the noise is severe, don't drive it. Never work under a car supported only by a jack.

How to prevent it

  • Check pad thickness at each service or tire change
  • Replace pads before they reach the metal
  • Change brake fluid on schedule
  • Clean and lubricate caliper slide pins during pad changes
  • Drive the car regularly, as long periods of standing cause rust

After new pads, a different noise can appear; read Brakes Squealing After New Pads?

FAQ

Is it safe to drive with grinding brakes?

Not if the noise is deep and constant. Worn-out pads reduce stopping power and can damage the rotors.

Why do my brakes grind in the morning?

Often surface rust on the rotors after the car has stood overnight. It should clear after a few stops.

Can I just replace the pads if the rotors are scored?

If the rotors are deeply scored or below minimum thickness, they usually need replacing with the pads.

Why are my new brakes grinding?

They may need bedding in, or there may be debris, a fitting problem or a damaged rotor.

How much does it cost to fix grinding brakes?

It depends on whether you need pads only or pads and rotors. Get a quote for your car.
