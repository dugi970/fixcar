How to Check Tire Pressure Correctly (and Where to Find the Right PSI)

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Check tire pressure when the tires are cold, with a reliable gauge, and compare it to the figure on the sticker in the driver's door frame or in the owner's manual, not the number on the tire sidewall. Adjust each tire, refit the valve caps and check monthly and before long trips.

Why tire pressure matters

Correct pressure improves grip, braking, handling and tire life, and helps fuel economy. Too low and the tire overheats and wears on the edges. Too high and you lose grip and comfort, and the center wears faster. Tires also lose pressure naturally over time and when the weather turns cold.

Where to find the right PSI

  • The sticker on the driver's door frame, or inside the fuel flap
  • The owner's manual, often with different values for light and heavy loads
  • Not the sidewall: that number is the tire's maximum, not the recommended pressure

Pressure is shown in psi, bar or kPa. Many cars use something in the region of 30 to 35 psi (2.1 to 2.4 bar), but yours may differ, so use your own figure.

How to check tire pressure, step by step

  1. Check when the tires are cold: before driving, or after the car has stood for a few hours.
  2. Remove the valve cap and press the gauge on firmly until the hissing stops.
  3. Read the figure and compare it with the recommended value.
  4. Add air or let some out in small steps, then re-check.
  5. Refit the valve caps to keep dirt out.
  6. Check the spare, if you have one.

Safety note. If a tire has a bulge, cut or keeps losing pressure, don't just keep topping it up. Have it inspected. Don't inflate beyond the tire's maximum pressure.

Why cold readings matter

Driving heats the air in the tires, which raises the pressure by several psi. A hot reading looks higher than it really is, so you could wrongly let air out. If you have to check hot, note the figure but don't release air. Top up if it's below the recommended cold pressure.

Common mistakes

  • Using the sidewall number instead of the door-sticker value
  • Checking hot tires
  • Relying on a cheap, uncalibrated gauge
  • Forgetting the spare
  • Ignoring the tire pressure warning light
  • Not adjusting for a heavy load or towing, when the manual says to

What the tire pressure warning light means

The tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS) light comes on when a tire is notably low. Check all four tires with a gauge, inflate to the recommended value, and drive for a few minutes. If the light stays on or flashes, a sensor may be faulty. A cold snap commonly triggers it. See also Check Engine Light: Flashing vs Steady for other warning lights.

FAQ

What is the correct tire pressure?

It's the figure on the door sticker or in your manual for your car and load. It isn't the sidewall maximum.

How often should I check tire pressure?

About once a month and before long trips or heavy loads.

Should I check tire pressure when the tires are hot?

Cold is best. Hot readings are higher and can mislead you.

Is it okay to use a gas station air pump?

Yes, but verify with your own gauge, as station gauges can be inaccurate.
