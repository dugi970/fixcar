How Often Should You Change Your Engine Oil?

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Follow your owner's manual first. Many modern cars using synthetic oil go roughly 10,000 to 15,000 km (6,000 to 10,000 miles) or about a year between changes, while conventional oil often needs changing sooner. Short trips, towing, dust and extreme heat or cold mean you should change it earlier.

How often to change engine oil

There isn't one number for every car. The right interval depends on the engine, the oil type and how you drive. The most reliable source is your service schedule, which gives a distance and a time limit, whichever comes first.

Oil typeTypical interval (general guide)Notes
ConventionalAbout 5,000 to 8,000 km (3,000 to 5,000 miles)Mostly older engines
Semi-syntheticAbout 8,000 to 12,000 km (5,000 to 7,500 miles)A middle option
Fully syntheticAbout 10,000 to 15,000 km (6,000 to 10,000 miles)Common on modern engines

These are general ranges only. Your manufacturer's figure takes priority.

What your owner's manual says

Many cars have a service indicator that calculates the interval from how the engine has been used. Others use a fixed schedule. If the manual lists a 'normal' and a 'severe' schedule, work out which one matches your driving. Use only the oil grade and approval shown there. See How to Read an Oil Dipstick to check the level between changes.

When to change oil sooner

  • Mostly short trips where the engine rarely warms up fully
  • Frequent stop-start traffic
  • Towing or carrying heavy loads
  • Very hot, cold or dusty conditions
  • Track days or hard driving
  • A low mileage car that does fewer than 10,000 km a year, because oil ages with time

Signs your oil needs changing

  • The service reminder or oil warning light appears
  • Oil on the dipstick is thick, gritty or smells burnt
  • The engine is noisier than usual
  • The oil level is dropping

Dark oil alone doesn't mean it is worn out. See Why Is My Oil Black Right After an Oil Change?

What happens if you wait too long?

Old oil loses its ability to lubricate and hold dirt in suspension. Sludge can build up, wear increases and the engine can run hotter. In the worst case, it can lead to serious damage. Changing the oil with a good filter (see Oil Filter: How Often to Change It) is cheap insurance. To do it yourself, read How to Change Engine Oil at Home.

FAQ

Is it okay to change oil once a year?

Yes, if you do low mileage. Oil ages even when the car sits, so most schedules include a time limit.

Can I go longer than the recommended interval?

It isn't advisable. Stick to the manual unless you have a good reason.

Does synthetic oil last longer?

Generally yes, as it resists breakdown better, but you still need to follow your manual.

Should I change oil before winter?

Not necessarily. Change it by the schedule, and use the right viscosity for your climate.

Do I change the filter every time?

Yes. A fresh filter keeps new oil clean.
