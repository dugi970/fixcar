How to Tighten Lug Nuts Properly (Torque and Pattern)

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Tighten lug nuts by hand first, lower the car, then use a torque wrench in a criss-cross (star) pattern to the value in your owner's manual. Re-check the torque after 50 to 100 km (30 to 60 miles). Never use an impact wrench for the final tightening, and never work under a car supported only by a jack.

Why lug nut torque matters

Loose lug nuts can let a wheel wobble or even come off. Over-tightened nuts can stretch studs, warp brake rotors and make the nuts almost impossible to remove later. The correct torque is a specific number for your vehicle, so find it in the owner's manual.

What you need

  • Torque wrench that covers your car's specification
  • Lug wrench or breaker bar
  • Socket that fits your nuts or bolts
  • Hydraulic jack and jack stands
  • Wheel chocks
  • Wire brush for hub and stud threads

Step by step

  1. Park on level, solid ground, parking brake on, in gear or Park, and chock the opposite wheels.
  2. Loosen the nuts about a half turn while the wheel is still on the ground.
  3. Raise the car at the jacking points shown in your manual and support it on jack stands.
  4. Remove the wheel. Clean the hub face and check that studs and nuts are undamaged.
  5. Fit the wheel and thread each nut by hand until snug.
  6. Lower the car until the tire just touches the ground, then tighten lightly in a star pattern.
  7. Lower the car fully and tighten with the torque wrench in a star pattern, in two stages if you like.
  8. Re-check after 50 to 100 km (30 to 60 miles).

Safety note. Never get under a car held only by a jack, and never rely on the jack alone for any job. Use rated jack stands on solid ground.

The correct tightening pattern

On a five-stud wheel, tighten the nuts in a star pattern, skipping one each time. On four and six-stud wheels, tighten opposite nuts in turn. This pulls the wheel evenly onto the hub and avoids a tilted fit.

What torque should you use?

Torque varies by vehicle, stud size and wheel type. Many passenger cars fall somewhere in the range of 80 to 120 Nm (60 to 90 lb-ft), but that's only a general idea. Use the figure in your manual, not a guess. If you've just had alloy wheels refitted, follow the manufacturer's advice on re-torquing.

Common mistakes

  • Using an impact wrench for final tightening
  • Oiling or greasing threads when the manual says to keep them dry
  • Tightening nuts in a circle instead of a star
  • Forgetting to re-check after a short drive
  • Cross-threading or mixing nut types
  • Ignoring dirt or rust on the hub face

While the wheel is off, it's a good time to look at the brakes. See Brake Pads vs Rotors and Why Do My Brakes Vibrate When Braking?. Uneven torque is one cause of vibration. Afterwards, check pressures with our tire pressure guide.

FAQ

How tight should lug nuts be?

Tight to the torque in your owner's manual, using a torque wrench. There's no universal figure.

Should I use an impact wrench on lug nuts?

It can loosen them, but use a torque wrench for the final tightening to avoid over-tightening.

Do I need to re-torque after changing a wheel?

Yes. Check again after 50 to 100 km (30 to 60 miles), especially with alloy wheels.

What happens if lug nuts are too loose?

The wheel can vibrate, damage the studs and, in the worst case, come off. Fix it before driving.
