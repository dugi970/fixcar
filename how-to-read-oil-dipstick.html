How to Read an Oil Dipstick and Check Your Engine Oil Level

By Dima · Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 5 min read

Quick answer

Park on level ground, switch the engine off for a few minutes, pull the dipstick, wipe it, push it fully back in and pull it out again. The oil should sit between MIN and MAX. Top up in small amounts with the grade in your manual and never overfill. Milky or gritty oil means a problem, so get it checked.

Checking your oil takes about two minutes and can save an engine. Most people either skip it or read the dipstick wrong. Here's how to do it properly.

Before you start

  • Park on level ground. A slope will give a false reading.
  • Turn the engine off and wait a few minutes so the oil drains back into the sump. Your manual may give a specific time.
  • Have a clean rag or paper towel ready.

Does your car have a dipstick? Some newer cars, including many recent BMWs, have no dipstick and check the oil level electronically through the dashboard or menu. If that's your car, use the method in your manual.

Step by step

  1. Pull the dipstick all the way out. It usually has a bright yellow or orange handle.
  2. Wipe it clean from top to bottom.
  3. Push it fully back in until it seats.
  4. Pull it out again and hold it level.
  5. Read where the oil film ends on the marked section.

How to read the marks

Most dipsticks have two marks, or a cross-hatched area, labelled MIN and MAX (or L and H). The oil should be between them.

  • At or near MAX: good.
  • Midway: fine, but keep an eye on it.
  • At or below MIN: top up soon.

The gap between the marks is often around one litre or quart, but this varies by engine, so check your manual before adding a lot.

Dark oil isn't automatically bad; see Why Is My Oil Black Right After an Oil Change?. Milky oil can mean coolant is involved, so compare with Car Overheating: Causes and What to Do.

What colour and texture tell you

Amber to light brown

Fresh to fairly fresh oil.

Dark brown or black

Common as oil ages and not always a problem on its own. If it's also overdue for a change, change it.

Milky or creamy

This can mean coolant is mixing with the oil, which is serious. Don't keep driving. Get the car looked at.

Gritty or with metal flecks

Rub a little between your fingers. If it feels gritty or has shiny particles, have it checked.

Not sure which oil to buy? Engine Oil Viscosity Explained explains the numbers on the bottle, and How Often Should You Change Your Engine Oil? covers the interval.

How to top up

  1. Check your manual for the right oil grade and specification. Using the wrong oil can cause problems.
  2. Remove the oil filler cap on the engine.
  3. Add a small amount, around a quarter litre at a time, using a funnel.
  4. Wait a minute, then re-check the dipstick.
  5. Stop when the level is below MAX, then refit the cap.

Why not overfill?

Too much oil is also harmful. The crankshaft can whip it into foam, which lubricates poorly and can push oil into places it shouldn't go. If you overfill by a lot, don't drive on it. Have the extra drained.

How often to check

Once a month is a sensible habit, and before any long trip. Older cars and some engines burn a little oil between changes, so checking often is worth it. If you're topping up noticeably between services, find out why.

Quick checklist

  • Level ground, engine off for a few minutes
  • Wipe, reinsert, pull, read
  • Between MIN and MAX
  • Right grade of oil from the manual
  • Add a little at a time

FAQ

Should the engine be hot or cold when I check the oil?

Either works if you wait a few minutes after switching off so the oil drains back. Always park on level ground.

How much oil is there between MIN and MAX?

Often around a litre, but it varies, so check your manual.

What if the oil level is above MAX?

A little over is usually tolerable, but a lot over can cause foaming. Have the excess drained.

My car has no dipstick. What now?

Many newer cars check the oil electronically through the dashboard or menu. Use the method in your manual.
