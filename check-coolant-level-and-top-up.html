How to Check Coolant Level and Top It Up Safely

By Dima · Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

With the engine fully cold, find the translucent coolant expansion tank and check the level sits between MIN and MAX. If it is low, top up with the coolant type specified in your manual (usually a 50/50 mix) to the MAX line. If it keeps dropping, there is a leak that needs checking.

Safety note. Never open the cap on a hot engine. The system is pressurised and can spray scalding coolant. Antifreeze is also toxic, so keep it away from children and pets.

How to check the coolant level

  1. Park on level ground and let the engine cool for several hours.
  2. Open the bonnet and find the coolant tank. It's a plastic tank with MIN and MAX marks, often with a symbol of a thermometer.
  3. Read the level against the marks. It should be between them.

Which coolant to use

Use the type in your manual. Don't pick by colour alone, because colours differ between makers. Mixing incompatible types can cause sludge and corrosion. Coolant is usually mixed 50/50 with distilled water, and many types are sold ready mixed, which is the simplest choice. Check the label.

How to top it up

  1. Wait until the engine is cold, then slowly open the cap.
  2. Add the correct coolant gradually to the MAX line, not above it.
  3. Refit the cap until it's tight.
  4. Run the engine, let it warm up, then check again when cold.

In an emergency, plain water gets you home, but it dilutes protection against freezing and corrosion. Have it corrected afterwards.

If the temperature gauge is already climbing, follow Car Overheating: Causes and What to Do Right Now first.

Signs the coolant is low

  • The temperature gauge climbs or the warning light comes on
  • The heater blows cold air
  • A sweet smell from the engine bay
  • Puddles under the car, often green, pink, blue or orange
  • A low coolant message on the dashboard

To identify a leak by colour and location, see Fluid Leaking Under Your Car: What Each Colour Means. White exhaust smoke is explained in Exhaust Smoke Colours Explained, and a squealing belt can point to the water pump (see this guide).

If the level keeps dropping

Coolant doesn't get used up, so a falling level means it's going somewhere. Look for wet hoses, a crusty residue around joints, or a leaking radiator or water pump. White exhaust smoke or milky oil can mean a head gasket problem, which needs a professional.

FAQ

Can I use just water?

Only as a short-term emergency fix.

How often should I check it?

Monthly, and before long trips. Replace it at the interval in your service schedule.

What colour is coolant?

Usually green, orange, pink or blue, depending on the type. Colour alone isn't a reliable guide, so use the type in your manual.

Why does my coolant level keep dropping?

A leak or, less often, a head gasket problem. Coolant isn't used up, so find where it's going.

Is it okay to top up coolant when the engine is hot?

No. Wait until it's cold, because the system is pressurised.
