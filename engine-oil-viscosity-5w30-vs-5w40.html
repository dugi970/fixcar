Engine Oil Viscosity Explained: 5W-30 vs 5W-40 and More

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

In a grade like 5W-30, the first number (with W for winter) shows how well the oil flows when cold, and the second shows how thick it is at operating temperature. Use exactly the grade and approval listed in your owner's manual. 5W-40 is slightly thicker when hot than 5W-30, but thicker is not automatically better.

What do the numbers on oil mean?

Viscosity describes how easily oil flows. A multigrade oil such as 5W-30 behaves like a thinner oil when cold and a thicker oil when hot.

  • First number and W: cold-start flow. The lower the number, the better it flows in the cold.
  • Second number: thickness at normal operating temperature. A higher number means thicker oil when hot.

5W-30 vs 5W-40

GradeCold flowHot thicknessTypical notes
0W-20Very goodThinCommon on newer, fuel-economy engines
5W-30GoodMediumVery common general-purpose grade
5W-40GoodThickerOften specified for some turbo and performance engines
10W-40ModerateThickerOlder engines, warmer climates

Both 5W-30 and 5W-40 flow the same in the cold, but the 40 holds a thicker film when hot. That's not necessarily better, because oil that is too thick can reduce efficiency and may not suit the engine's tolerances.

Viscosity isn't the only thing to check

Your manual also lists approvals or specifications, such as ACEA, API or a manufacturer standard. These define additives, deposit control and compatibility with parts such as diesel particulate filters. An oil can have the right viscosity and still be wrong if it lacks the approval your engine needs.

How to choose the right oil

  1. Check your owner's manual for the viscosity grade and approval.
  2. Look at the badge or the oil cap, which sometimes shows the grade.
  3. Match both the viscosity and the specification on the bottle.
  4. Consider your climate only within the options the manufacturer allows.
  5. If your car is old or high mileage, ask a knowledgeable mechanic before changing the grade.

Can I mix or switch oils?

A small top-up with a different brand in an emergency is generally safer than running low, but it's best to use the same grade. Switching to a different grade is not recommended unless your manual allows it. When changing oil, follow How to Change Engine Oil at Home, and check the level using How to Read an Oil Dipstick.

What happens if you use the wrong viscosity?

  • Too thin: may reduce protection under load or at high temperatures.
  • Too thick: slower cold flow, harder starting and possibly higher fuel use.
  • Wrong specification: can harm emission systems or void a warranty.

For service timing, see How Often Should You Change Your Engine Oil? and Oil Filter: How Often to Change It.

FAQ

Can I use 5W-40 instead of 5W-30?

Only if your manufacturer approves it. Otherwise stick to the specified grade.

What does the W in 5W-30 mean?

W stands for winter. It refers to the oil's performance in cold conditions.

Is thicker oil better for an old engine?

Not automatically. Some high-mileage engines may benefit, but follow the manual or get advice.

Is 0W-20 too thin?

Not if your engine is designed for it. It's common on modern engines.

Does oil colour tell me the viscosity?

No. Colour says little. See Why Is My Oil Black Right After an Oil Change?
