Tire Tread Depth: How to Check It and When to Replace

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Most countries set a legal minimum of about 1.6 mm (2/32 inch) of tread, but grip in the wet drops well before that, so many experts suggest replacing tires at around 3 mm. Check with a tread depth gauge, the wear indicator bars in the grooves or a coin, and look for uneven wear that points to pressure, alignment or suspension problems.

Why tread depth matters

Tread channels water away from the contact patch. As the tread wears, the tire is more likely to aquaplane, and braking distances in the wet get longer. Tread depth also matters in snow and slush. Check it regularly, not just at inspection time.

How to check tread depth

Tread depth gauge

A cheap gauge gives the most accurate reading. Push the probe into a main groove and read the depth. Measure in several places across the tire.

Wear indicator bars

Most tires have small raised bars in the grooves. When the tread is level with them, the tire is at the legal limit.

Coin test

Insert a coin into the groove. In the US, a penny with Lincoln's head upside down is used to check 2/32 inch. In other countries, local coin tests exist for 1.6 mm. A coin is only a rough guide.

How much tread is enough?

Tread depthMeaning
8 mm (about 10/32 in) or moreNew tire
4 mm or moreGood for wet and winter driving
3 mm (about 4/32 in)Consider replacing soon, especially for wet or winter use
1.6 mm (2/32 in)Legal minimum in many countries. Replace now
Below the legal minimumIllegal and unsafe

Some countries have a higher minimum for winter tires, so check your local rules.

Wear patterns and what they mean

PatternLikely cause
Center wornOver-inflation
Both edges wornUnder-inflation. See How to Check Tire Pressure
One edge wornAlignment problem
Cupping or scallopingWorn shocks or suspension. May also cause steering wheel shake
Flat spotsHeavy braking or locked wheels

Tire age and damage

Rubber ages even if the tread looks fine. The four-digit DOT date code on the sidewall shows the week and year of manufacture. Many manufacturers advise having tires of about six years or older inspected regularly, and replacing them by around ten years. Also replace any tire with cuts, bulges or exposed cords.

Safety note. Tires are the only contact between your car and the road. Replace worn or damaged tires promptly, and don't mix very different tires on the same axle.

After fitting new tires

  • Check the pressures, using the door sticker value
  • Re-check the lug nut torque after a short drive. See How to Tighten Lug Nuts Properly
  • Get the alignment checked if you saw uneven wear

FAQ

What is the legal minimum tread depth?

Commonly 1.6 mm (2/32 inch), though it varies by country. Check your local rules.

When should I replace tires?

Replace by the legal limit at the latest. Many people choose around 3 mm for better wet grip.

Does tread depth affect fuel economy?

Slightly, but tire pressure has a bigger effect.

Can I replace just two tires?

Yes, but fit the new ones on the rear axle for stability, and follow your tire shop's advice.

How do I read the DOT date code?

The last four digits show the week and year, for example 2423 means week 24 of 2023.
