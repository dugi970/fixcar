How to Test a Car Battery With a Multimeter (and What the Numbers Mean)

By Dima · Published November 3, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Set a multimeter to DC volts (20 V range), connect red to the positive (+) and black to the negative (−) terminal with the engine off. A healthy, rested battery reads about 12.6 V or more. Around 12.2 V is half charged, and below about 12.0 V is flat. With the engine running, the reading should rise to roughly 13.7 to 14.7 V if the alternator is charging.

What you need

  • A basic digital multimeter
  • Safety glasses and gloves
  • A wire brush or cloth to clean the terminals
  • A helper for the cranking test, if you want to do it

Take off rings and watches, switch off everything electrical and keep metal tools away from the battery terminals. If the car won't start at all, read Car Clicks But Won't Start: Battery or Starter? first.

How to test a car battery with a multimeter

  1. Switch the car off and leave it for at least a few hours, or switch on the headlights for a minute and then off to remove surface charge.
  2. Set the multimeter to DC volts (V with a straight line), on the 20 V range.
  3. Touch the red probe to the positive (+) terminal and the black probe to the negative (−) terminal. Make sure the contact is on clean metal.
  4. Read the voltage and compare it with the table below.

Safety note. Never let the probes or a tool bridge the two terminals. A battery can deliver a large current and cause burns or sparks. Keep flames and sparks away from the battery.

What the voltage readings mean

Reading (engine off, rested)Approx. chargeWhat it suggests
12.6 V or higherAbout 100%Healthy and fully charged
About 12.4 VAbout 75%Fine, could use a longer drive
About 12.2 VAbout 50%Needs charging
About 12.0 VAbout 25%Very low. Charge and re-test
Below 11.8 VFlatPossibly damaged or sulfated

These are general guides for a standard 12-volt lead-acid battery. AGM and start-stop batteries can read a little differently, so check the battery label or manual.

Cranking and charging test

Cranking test

Have a helper start the engine while you watch the meter. The voltage will drop, but it should normally stay above about 9.6 V. A drop far below that suggests a weak battery.

Charging test

With the engine running, the voltage across the battery should be roughly 13.7 to 14.7 V. If it stays near 12 V or lower, the alternator may not be charging. See Battery Light On While Driving. A reading much above 15 V can also indicate a charging fault.

If you need to start the car in the meantime, see How to Jump-Start a Car Safely.

How long do car batteries last?

Most last roughly three to five years, depending on climate, driving pattern and battery quality. Short trips, extreme heat, corroded terminals and parasitic drains shorten life. If your battery is over four years old and the readings are marginal, replacing it is often cheaper than a breakdown.

FAQ

What should a car battery read on a multimeter?

About 12.6 V or more when rested and fully charged, and roughly 13.7 to 14.7 V with the engine running.

Can a battery read 12 V and still be bad?

Yes. A weak battery can show a normal-looking voltage at rest but fail under load, which is why a cranking test helps.

Can I test a battery while it's in the car?

Yes, you can test across the terminals without removing it.

How do I check for a parasitic drain?

It needs a multimeter in current (amp) mode in series with the battery lead. It's easy to get wrong, so follow your manual or ask a professional.

Does cold weather affect battery voltage?

Yes. Cold reduces capacity and makes engines harder to crank, so weak batteries often fail in winter.
