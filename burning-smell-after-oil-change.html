Burning Smell After an Oil Change: Is It Normal?

By Dima · Published November 1, 2026 · Updated November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

A faint burning smell for the first drive or two after an oil change is common. Oil spilled on the exhaust or engine burns off. If the smell is strong, lasts more than a short drive or two, you see smoke, or the oil level drops, check for a leak from the filter or drain plug and stop driving if it gets worse.

After an oil change I always look around the filter, the drain plug and the exhaust before calling the job done. Even a small amount of spilled oil can smell surprisingly strong once the engine gets hot. If the smell doesn't fade after a short drive, I go looking for a leak rather than assuming it's just leftover oil.

Why does it smell after an oil change?

When oil is poured or drips off the filter, a little usually lands on the exhaust manifold or on engine parts. Once they heat up, it burns off with a sharp, acrid smell and sometimes a thin wisp of smoke. A small amount disappears within a short drive.

Normal or a problem?

  • Normal: faint smell, fades in one or two drives, no drips, oil level steady.
  • Problem: strong smell, visible smoke, drips on the ground, oil light, or falling oil level.

If you did the change yourself, How to Change Engine Oil at Home lists the steps where leaks usually start, and Oil Filter: How Often to Change It explains how to seat a filter properly.

Common causes of a real problem

A loose or badly sealed oil filter

If the filter wasn't tightened enough, or the old gasket stayed stuck to the engine so there are two, oil leaks out and burns.

A loose drain plug or a missing washer

The drain plug seals with a washer. A missing, flat or reused washer, or a plug that's loose, can weep oil.

Cartridge housing seals

On cartridge-type filters, a pinched or missing O-ring or an incorrectly torqued cap leaks.

A loose filler cap

If the filler cap isn't on properly, oil can splash out while driving.

Overfilling

Too much oil can foam and be pushed out of vents or seals. Check the level.

What to check

  1. Let the engine cool, then look under the car for fresh oil drips or a wet patch.
  2. Check the oil filter and drain plug for wetness and grime.
  3. Check the filler cap is tight.
  4. Read the dipstick on level ground. Is it between MIN and MAX?
  5. Wipe visible oil off the exhaust and engine with degreaser so you can see if it returns.

Other smells have other causes: a sweet smell points to coolant (see Fluid Leaking Under Your Car), and a slipping belt can smell of burning rubber (see Squealing Noise When Starting a Cold Car).

Is it another smell?

  • Burning rubber: a slipping belt or a hose touching something hot.
  • Sweet, syrupy: coolant leak.
  • Burning brakes: a caliper that's sticking.

Safety note. If you see heavy smoke, the oil pressure light comes on, or the level drops fast, switch off the engine and don't drive further.

FAQ

How long does the smell last?

Usually only a short drive or two. If it's still there after a few days, look for a leak.

Can I drive with a leak?

Low oil can destroy an engine. Fix the leak first, and keep checking the level.

Why does my car smell of burning oil after an oil change?

Oil spilled on the exhaust or engine burns off as it heats up. It should fade within a drive or two.

Can a loose oil filter cause a burning smell?

Yes. A loose filter, or one with a doubled seal, leaks onto hot parts. Check it once the engine is cool.
